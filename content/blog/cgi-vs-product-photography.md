Where 3D renders save time and money, and where a photo shoot is still the better call.

## When CGI wins

- The product is not manufactured yet
- You have many colour or size variants
- Glass, chrome or liquids that are hard to shoot cleanly
- You need the same angles again for future products
- Exploded views and cutaways

## When photography wins

- Food, fabric and skin, where real texture is the point
- People using the product in real places
- A one-off shoot of a single finished product

## Cost over time

A 3D model is built once. Every later shot, variant or animation starts from it, so the cost per image drops as the range grows.

## Mixing both

Many brands shoot lifestyle images with people and use CGI for packshots and variants. The two sit well together when lighting is matched.
